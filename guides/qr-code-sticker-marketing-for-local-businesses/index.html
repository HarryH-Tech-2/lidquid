QR code sticker marketing for local businesses: a practical playbook

By the Lidquid team · Updated October 16, 2026 · 4 min read

the short answer

QR sticker marketing works when four things line up: a dynamic QR code you can track, a placement where people have time to look, an offer worth pulling out a phone for, and a fast mobile page that delivers it. Get those right and every scan becomes a measurable customer touchpoint.

  • Use dynamic codes so you can count scans and change the destination.
  • Measure cost per scan and what happens after the scan.

Should you use a static or a dynamic QR code?

Use a dynamic QR code for anything you want to measure. A static code permanently encodes one URL and can't report scans, while a dynamic code points to a short redirect you control, so you can count scans and change where it goes after printing.

Static QRDynamic QR
CostFreePaid tools commonly run about $5–$50 a month
Scan countsNoYes, usually with time and rough location
Change destination after printingNo, you'd need to reprintYes
Best forWi-Fi details, permanent menusCampaigns, offers, anything you'll optimize

If you already use a link tool, you can get much of the same benefit by pointing a static code at a short link you control and adding UTM parameters, so visits show up properly in your analytics.

Where should you put QR stickers?

Put them where people have both time and a reason to look: places with dwell time, at eye level, within a few feet of the viewer. A code people see while walking past at speed rarely gets scanned.

  • Counters and tables in your own premises, where people wait or sit.
  • Partner businesses: a nearby café, gym or bookshop that shares your customers.
  • Packaging and bags, so the code goes home with the customer.
  • Event spaces and community boards, where permitted.
  • Laptop lids in cafés, libraries and coworking spaces, where people sit near each other for hours (more below).
Don't fly-post. Sticking codes on public property or other people's businesses without permission can get you fined and damages your brand. Ask first, every time.

What offer makes people scan?

A specific, immediate payoff. "Scan for 15% off today" beats "Scan to learn more" because the person knows exactly what they get for the effort of unlocking their phone.

  • Local services: free first class, free trial session, a free add-on with a first booking.
  • Food and drink: a free pastry or upgrade with the next order, redeemable this week.
  • Retail and DTC: a first-order discount code that applies automatically on the landing page.
  • Apps: a sign-up bonus, or a direct deep link to the app store.

Curiosity also works, especially in social settings. A playful line like "what is this?" or "scan this, I get paid" gets scans on its own, but pair it with a real offer on the landing page or the curiosity goes to waste.

What should the landing page look like?

It should be built for a phone, load in a second or two, and deliver the promise from the sticker above the fold. Most QR traffic is on mobile data, standing up, with one hand.

  • Repeat the sticker's promise as the headline, so people know they're in the right place.
  • Show the offer or code immediately. Don't hide it behind a long form.
  • Ask for one thing: an email, a booking or a download. Not five.
  • Add tracking (UTM parameters or a dedicated page) so you can tell sticker traffic from everything else.
  • Keep large images compressed and skip pop-ups that cover the offer.

How do you measure QR sticker results?

Track three numbers: scans, conversions after the scan, and cost. Divide cost by scans for your cost per scan, and cost by conversions for your cost per customer.

cost per scan = total campaign cost ÷ scans
cost per customer = total campaign cost ÷ customers from those scans

Use a separate code or link per placement, such as one for the counter, one for the partner café and one for packaging, so you can see which spot actually earns its keep. Scan counts from basic tools can include repeat scans from the same phone, so treat them as an upper bound. What should you know about printing QR stickers?

Print the code big enough, with high contrast and a clear border, on material suited to where it'll live. Most scanning failures come from codes that are too small, too low-contrast or covered by design elements.

  • Size for the distance. A larger code scans from further away. Test at the real viewing distance before ordering in bulk.
  • Contrast. Dark modules on a light background. Inverted or pastel codes fail on some phones.
  • Quiet zone. Leave empty space around the code and keep artwork off the modules.
  • Material. Removable vinyl for surfaces you don't own, including laptops. Weatherproof vinyl for outdoors. Laminate if it'll be touched a lot.
  • Test a physical proof. Scan the printed sticker with several phones, iPhone and Android, before the full run.

Questions people ask

How much does a dynamic QR code cost?
Paid dynamic QR tools commonly cost about $5 to $50 a month, depending on scan volume and features. Static codes are free but can't track scans or change destination.
Do QR codes still work for marketing in 2026?
Yes, when there's a clear reason to scan and the placement gives people time to do it. Phones scan QR codes from the built-in camera, so there's no app to install.
How big should a QR sticker be?
Big enough to scan from the real viewing distance. Codes seen from across a table need to be larger than codes on a product in someone's hand, so test a printed proof at the actual distance.
Can I put QR stickers anywhere in public?
No. Only place stickers where you have permission. Fly-posting on public property or other businesses can lead to fines and damages your reputation.
How do I know which sticker placement is working?
Use a separate dynamic code or tracked link for each placement, then compare scans and conversions per placement over the same period.
