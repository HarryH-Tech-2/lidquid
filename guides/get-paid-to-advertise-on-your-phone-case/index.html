Can you get paid to advertise on your phone case, water bottle or backpack?

By the Lidquid team · Updated October 19, 2026 · 4 min read

the short answer

Rarely, and almost never in a way that pays well. Brands pay to advertise on things that sit in front of lots of strangers for hours, like cars on a commute or a laptop open in a café. A phone case spends most of its life in a pocket, and a water bottle or backpack is usually only seen in passing, so there's little to measure. Be wary of any offer that wants money first.

Can you really get paid to advertise on a phone case, bottle or backpack?

Technically yes, but real programs are rare, and the ones that exist rarely pay much. Advertisers pay for attention they can count. If nobody can show that a stranger saw the ad, or did anything about it, there's nothing for the brand to pay for.

That's why car wraps became the standard "get paid to advertise" example: a car is seen by thousands of people on a commute, and the mileage can be tracked. Smaller items have a harder time making that case.

Why don't most small items pay?

Three things decide whether an item is worth advertising on: how long it's visible, how many strangers see it, and whether anyone can measure the result.

  • Phone case: it's in your hand briefly and in a pocket or bag the rest of the time. Few strangers see it, and a case is a poor surface for a scannable code.
  • Water bottle: it's visible, but it's small, curved and often on a desk or in a bag. Hard to scan and hard to verify.
  • Backpack: it faces away from the people behind you, and strangers glimpse it for seconds while you walk past.
  • Laptop lid: it faces the room for hours at a stretch in cafés, libraries and coworking spaces, and a flat lid suits a QR sticker.

Looked at that way, the laptop lid is the small item that does make sense. It's the reason getting paid to put a sticker on your laptop exists as a program at all.

What kinds of "advertise on your stuff" offers actually exist?

A few formats show up repeatedly, and they differ in who pays and what you're paid for.

  • Car wraps and decals: a brand covers part or all of your car and pays a monthly amount. It needs a car and regular driving. See our comparison with laptop stickers.
  • Sponsored laptop stickers: a removable QR sticker for a brand you approve, paid per verified scan. Lidquid is one of these, and it's still a pilot.
  • Free branded merch: a brand gives you a cap, bottle or bag and hopes you use it. That's a freebie, not income.
  • Influencer or ambassador deals: you're paid to post about a product. This needs an audience, and it isn't really "wearing" an ad.
  • Sticker-space auctions: a one-off sale of space on a device, usually by someone with a following.

Notice that almost none of these pay you for a phone case, bottle or backpack specifically. If a site claims they do, look closely at how you'd be paid.

How do you spot a scam?

Offers to pay you for advertising on everyday items attract scammers, because the idea sounds easy and nobody expects to be asked for a lot of information. Stop if you see any of these:

  • You're asked to pay a "registration", "kit" or "wrap" fee before you earn anything.
  • They send a cheque or transfer larger than agreed and ask you to forward the difference.
  • The pay is high and fixed, for a small item and no stated work.
  • They ask for bank login details, an ID scan or gift cards up front.
  • You can't find who the advertiser is, how scans or views are verified, or how you're paid.

A real program pays you, not the other way around. Our guide to passive income scams goes through the patterns in more detail, and is laptop sticker advertising legit? shows what to check for in this category.

What should you do with your phone case, bottle and backpack?

Keep them yours. Stickers and branding on small everyday items don't pay, so there's no reason to give up the look you like for a promise. If you want to earn from something you already own, think about what strangers actually see for a long time. For many people that's a laptop in a café, or a car on a daily commute.

  1. Pick the item that gets seen. Laptop lid, then car, then everything else.
  2. Check how you're paid before you apply: per scan, per month or per post?
  3. Never pay to start. Walk away from fees and cheques.
  4. Choose removable. You should be able to undo it whenever you like.

Questions people ask

Can you get paid to put an ad on your phone case?
Almost never. A phone case is rarely seen by strangers for long and is hard to scan or verify, so advertisers don't pay for it. Treat offers that claim otherwise with suspicion.
Do any companies pay you to wear branded clothing or carry a branded bag?
Mostly they give you free merchandise, which isn't income. Paid deals usually involve an audience and posting about the product.
What is the best item to advertise on?
Items strangers look at for a long time. A car on a daily commute and a laptop lid in a café are the two that real programs use.
Is it safe to put a sponsored sticker on my laptop?
A removable vinyl sticker generally peels off cleanly, but test a small corner first and avoid leaving it on for years. Read our guide on whether stickers are bad for your laptop for the details.
How do I know a get-paid-to-advertise offer is real?
A real program tells you who the advertiser is, how views or scans are verified and how you're paid, and never asks you to pay first or forward money from a cheque.
Does Lidquid pay for phone cases or bottles?
No. Lidquid only places removable QR stickers on laptop lids, paid per verified scan. It is a pilot that opens one city at a time, and it is free to join.
