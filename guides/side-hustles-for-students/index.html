Low-effort side hustles for students in 2026

By the Lidquid team · Updated October 16, 2026 · 4 min read

the short answer

The best side hustles for students fit around a timetable that changes every semester. Good low-effort options include tutoring, on-campus jobs, paid research studies, user testing, pet sitting, reselling, freelancing and laptop sticker advertising. The easiest pick depends on whether you're trading time (tutoring, campus jobs) or using something you already do (studying in public, owning a laptop).

What makes a side hustle good for a student?

A good student side hustle is flexible around classes and exams, needs little or no money up front, and doesn't eat the time you need for studying. Earnings matter, but a gig that collapses every finals week isn't much use.

When you compare options, ask four questions:

  • Effort: how much active work does each dollar take?
  • Flexibility: can you pause it during exams without losing it?
  • Startup cost: do you need to buy anything, or pay anyone, to begin?
  • Rules: does your school, visa or scholarship limit how you can earn?
International students: visa conditions often restrict paid work, sometimes including freelance or gig income. Check with your school's international student office before starting anything on this list.

What are the best low-effort side hustles for students?

Here are ten realistic options, roughly from most active to most passive. Earnings for all of them vary widely with your location, skills and how much time you put in, so we've described them honestly rather than quoting numbers we can't back up.

1. Tutoring

If you're strong in a subject, tutoring younger students or classmates is one of the better-paid hours available to students. It's skilled work, so it's not truly low-effort, but you set your own schedule and it builds your CV. Sessions can be online or in person.

2. On-campus jobs

Library desks, campus cafés, labs and front desks are built around student timetables. Employers expect exam-season schedule changes. Some desk jobs even let you study during quiet periods.

3. Selling study notes (where allowed)

Well-organized notes can sell to students who take the same course later. Check your university's rules first. Many schools restrict selling lecture materials or notes based on them, and breaking those rules can be an academic integrity issue.

4. Reselling

Buying underpriced items such as textbooks, furniture at move-out, vintage clothing and electronics, and reselling them online works well around a student calendar. Move-in and move-out weeks are natural peaks. It does need some cash up front and storage space.

5. User testing

Websites and apps pay people to try their products and talk through what's confusing. Tests are short and done from your laptop, but availability is unpredictable. Treat it as occasional extra money, not a steady income.

6. Freelancing

Design, writing, video editing, coding, social media management: if you have a skill, there's someone who'll pay for it. Freelancing can pay well and builds a portfolio, but finding clients takes effort and deadlines don't care about your exams.

7. Pet sitting and dog walking

Pet owners near campus often need help during the day or over holidays. It's flexible and gets you outside. Holiday pet sitting can be useful if you stay in town while others travel.

8. Paid research studies

Universities regularly recruit participants for psychology, health and economics studies, and many pay for your time. Check the psychology department's participant pages and campus noticeboards. Opportunities come and go, but each one is usually low-effort.

9. Campus brand ambassador

Some companies pay students to promote products on campus or on social media. It suits outgoing people with an active campus network. Read the terms carefully, because some ambassador programs pay mainly in free products or commission.

Which student side hustles are actually passive?

Very few are truly passive. The closest are ones that earn from something you'd do anyway, like laptop sticker advertising while you study in public, or notes you've already written (where your school allows selling them).

Be wary of anything sold as "passive income" that needs you to pay first: courses, starter kits, "investment" schemes or dropshipping bundles. The passive part usually benefits the person selling the course.

Can you combine more than one?

Yes, and pairing one active hustle with one passive one often works best. For example, an on-campus library job plus a Lidquid sticker on the laptop you study on means you're earning a little extra from hours you'd spend there anyway.

The trick is choosing hustles that don't compete for the same scarce resource. Two that both need your evenings will clash during exams. One that needs evenings and one that needs nothing won't.

How do you avoid student side hustle scams?

Students are a favorite target for scams because they're often short of cash and new to work. Walk away from any opportunity that:

  • asks you to pay a fee to start earning
  • sends you a cheque and asks you to send part of it back
  • wants your bank login or asks you to "receive and forward" money
  • promises a large guaranteed income for little or no work

Questions people ask

What is the easiest side hustle for a college student?
The easiest options earn from things you already do, such as paid research studies, occasional user testing, or laptop sticker advertising if you regularly study in public places. All of them pay less reliably than a regular job.
Can international students do side hustles?
It depends on your visa. Many student visas restrict paid work, sometimes including freelance or gig income. Check with your school's international student office before starting.
Is it allowed to sell my lecture notes?
Not always. Many universities restrict selling notes based on lecture materials. Check your school's academic integrity and intellectual property policies first.
Do I need followers to make money as a student?
No. Most of these side hustles, including tutoring, campus jobs, research studies and laptop sticker advertising with Lidquid, don't need any social media audience.
Are paid side hustles that ask for a sign-up fee legit?
Treat any side hustle that asks you to pay first with suspicion. Legitimate work pays you; it doesn't charge you to start.
How much can a student earn from laptop sticker advertising?
It depends on how many days your laptop is out in public and how many new people scan it. As an illustration only, 5 days a week, 3 scans a day at $0.50 a scan is about $32 a month. Lidquid is in pilot and hasn't published real earnings yet.
