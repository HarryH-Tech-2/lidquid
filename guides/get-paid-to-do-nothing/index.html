Can you really get paid to do nothing?

By the Lidquid team · Updated October 16, 2026 · 5 min read

the short answer

Almost, but never quite. Nothing pays you for literally zero effort. A few options pay you for something you already do or own, after a little setup: cashback, renting out unused space, sharing spare bandwidth, or a sponsored sticker on your laptop.

  • What's real: small, irregular amounts that vary widely.
  • What's hype: anything promising big, fixed money for doing nothing.
  • The rule: if it asks you to pay first, it isn't "free money".

Is it actually possible to get paid to do nothing?

Not in the literal sense. What you can do is get paid for doing something you'd do anyway, or for letting someone use something you aren't using. Every legitimate option on this page needs some setup, gives up something (attention, data, space or a bit of convenience), and pays an amount that depends heavily on your situation.

That framing matters because "get paid to do nothing" is also the hook behind a lot of scams. Before looking at real options, keep one test in mind: money should only ever flow to you. The moment a "do nothing" opportunity needs your money, your card details for a "verification fee" or a cheque you have to cash and partly send back, you're no longer the one being paid.

Which "do nothing" options are actually real?

These are the categories that hold up. None will replace a paycheck, but they can add small amounts for very little ongoing effort.

1. Cashback on purchases you'd make anyway

Cashback cards, browser extensions and shopping portals return a slice of what you spend. If you'd buy the groceries regardless, the cashback is about as close to "nothing" as it gets. The catch: it only works if it doesn't nudge you into spending more, and card cashback is worthless if you carry a balance and pay interest.

2. Renting out space or things you don't use

A spare parking spot, an empty garage, storage space, camera gear or a bike you rarely ride can all be rented through listing sites. The setup is a listing and some photos. The ongoing effort is handovers and the odd message. Check your lease, insurance and local rules first, because some landlords and HOAs prohibit subletting space. See passive income from things you already own for the full list.

3. Bandwidth-sharing apps

Some apps pay you to let companies route traffic through your spare home internet connection. Installing one takes minutes and it runs in the background. The trade-offs are real, though. Other people's traffic leaves through your IP address, it may break your internet provider's terms of service, and some of these apps have had security problems. If you try one, read its privacy policy, check your ISP terms, and never run it on a work network or work device.

4. Passive research and data panels

Some market-research panels pay you, usually in points or gift cards, to share anonymized browsing or purchase data, or to keep an app installed that measures media use. It's low effort but you're trading data, so read exactly what's collected and how to delete it. Avoid any panel that wants access to your banking apps or passwords.

6. Interest on savings you already have

If you keep cash in an account that pays little or nothing, moving it to an account that pays more interest is a one-time task that earns from then on. Rates change and accounts differ, so compare current terms. This is general information, not financial advice.

Notice the pattern: the closer something is to truly "nothing", the smaller and less predictable the payout. That's normal, not a flaw. Low effort and big money rarely come in the same package, and anything claiming both deserves suspicion.

What's hype?

A lot of "get paid to do nothing" content online is really marketing for something else. Be skeptical of:

  • Apps that pay you to "watch ads" or "play games" with huge advertised payouts. Many pay pennies, set high cash-out thresholds, or make it hard to ever withdraw.
  • "Passive income" courses. If the main way someone earns is by selling you the method, the method is the product.
  • "Done-for-you" stores or accounts that you buy and that supposedly earn on autopilot.
  • Fixed, high weekly payments for advertising on your car, laptop or clothes regardless of where you go. That's a common scam script.

What are the red flags?

Walk away from any "do nothing" offer that:

  • asks you to pay a fee, buy a starter kit or "unlock" earnings
  • sends you a cheque or payment and asks you to send part of it back
  • wants your online banking login, passwords or remote access to your device
  • guarantees a set income or promises returns with no risk
  • pressures you to decide fast or recruit friends to get paid
If it smells off, it probably is. Our guide to passive income scams covers the most common scripts and what to do if you've already engaged with one.

What's the smartest way to approach it?

Stack a few genuinely low-effort options that fit your life instead of chasing one big promise. Someone who already works from cafés could combine cashback on their usual spending, interest on existing savings, and a laptop sticker. That's three small streams with almost no extra time. Set each one up once, check it monthly, and drop anything that starts costing you more attention than it pays.

Questions people ask

Can you really make money doing nothing?
Not literally. You can earn small, irregular amounts from things you already do or own, like cashback, renting unused space or a sponsored laptop sticker, after some setup.
Are bandwidth-sharing apps safe?
They carry real risks. Other people's traffic uses your IP address, they may break your internet provider's terms, and some have had security issues. Read the privacy policy and never run one on a work network or device.
Do apps that pay you to watch ads actually pay?
Some do, but usually very little, often with high cash-out thresholds. Be wary of big advertised payouts.
Is Lidquid a way to get paid to do nothing?
It's close. Once the removable sticker is on your laptop, there's nothing to post or promote. You earn per verified scan while you work in public as usual. Earnings depend on how often your laptop is out.
Do I have to pay anything to get started?
Legitimate low-effort options don't charge you to start earning. Any 'do nothing' offer that asks for an upfront fee is a red flag.
Is this income taxable?
In most countries, yes. Income from side sources is generally taxable, so keep simple records. Check local rules or ask a tax professional.
