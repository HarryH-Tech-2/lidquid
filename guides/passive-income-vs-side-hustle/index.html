Passive income & side hustles

Passive income vs side hustle: what's the difference?

By the Lidquid team · Updated October 16, 2026 · 4 min read

the short answer

A side hustle trades your time for money. Passive income keeps paying after the work is done. In practice it's a spectrum. Most "passive" income needs real upfront work or money, plus some upkeep, and most side hustles can be made more passive over time.

  • Side hustle: you stop working, the money stops.
  • Passive income: you set it up once, then it earns with little attention.

What's the difference between passive income and a side hustle?

The difference is whether your earnings depend on your hours. With a side hustle, like freelancing, tutoring, delivery driving or pet sitting, you get paid for the time you put in. With passive income, like rental income, royalties or interest, the money keeps arriving without you working each hour for it.

Both are ways to earn outside your main job or studies. People often mix them up because "passive income" is used loosely online. Plenty of things marketed as passive are really side hustles with good branding.

Does "passive income" mean something different for taxes?

Yes. In everyday language, "passive income" just means money that doesn't need much ongoing work. Tax authorities can use a narrower definition. In the US, for example, the IRS has specific "passive activity" rules covering certain rental activities and businesses you don't materially participate in. Something can feel passive to you without counting as passive for tax purposes. This guide uses the everyday meaning. For anything tax-related, check official guidance or ask a tax professional. Is income ever fully passive?

Rarely. It's more useful to think of a spectrum from fully active to mostly passive:

TypeExamplesUpfront effortOngoing effort
Fully activeDelivery driving, babysitting, hourly freelancingLowHigh: no work, no pay
Active, scalableFreelance retainers, tutoring groups, resellingMediumMedium
Build once, maintainDigital products, a blog or YouTube channel, templatesHighLow to medium
Asset-basedRenting space or gear, interest on savingsMoney or assets neededLow
Near-passiveCashback on normal spending, a sponsored laptop stickerLowVery low

Notice the trade-off. The more passive an option is, the more it usually needs either a lot of upfront work, or money and assets you already have. The rare exceptions, things that are both low effort and need no money, tend to pay small, variable amounts.

What do people get wrong about passive income?

The biggest misconception is that passive means effortless from day one. Most passive streams front-load the work, so you put in effort for weeks or months before seeing much back, if anything.

  • "Set it and forget it." Blogs need updating, rentals need handovers and repairs, digital products need support and marketing. Passive usually means less upkeep, not none.
  • "It scales automatically." Some streams scale, but many hit a ceiling set by your assets, your audience or your local market.
  • "Anyone can do it with no money." No-money options exist, but they tend to pay small amounts. The bigger passive streams usually need capital, a large audience or a valuable asset.
  • "It's safer than a job." Passive income can stop without warning when a platform changes its rules, a tenant leaves or demand dries up.

None of this means passive income isn't worth building. It means you should judge each option on effort, cost and risk, not on the word "passive" in its marketing.

Which is better: passive income or a side hustle?

Neither is better in general. It depends on what you have more of: time, money or existing assets.

A side hustle makes sense if…

  • you need money soon and can't wait months for something to grow
  • you have time and a skill but little money to start with
  • you want predictable pay tied to effort

Passive income makes sense if…

  • you're short on time but can invest effort upfront
  • you already own something useful: space, gear, savings or an audience
  • you're fine with irregular, slower-building earnings

Earnings vary widely in both, and anyone promising a specific income without knowing your situation is guessing, or selling something. Our guide to passive income scams covers the hype to avoid.

How can you combine a side hustle with passive income?

The most practical approach for most people is to use active work to fund or build passive streams, and to layer near-passive options on top of what you already do.

  1. Start active. A side hustle like tutoring or freelancing brings in money now and builds skills.
  2. Package what you know. Turn repeat work into something reusable, like a template, a guide or a recorded lesson, where your school or clients allow it.
  3. Add near-passive layers. Cashback on spending you'd do anyway, interest on savings you already have, or a sponsored laptop sticker if you work in public.
  4. Review monthly. Drop anything that eats more time or attention than it returns.

How do you decide what to start with?

Start with what you have the most of. If it's time, pick a side hustle. If it's an asset you aren't using, rent it out. If it's existing habits, like spending, saving or working in public, add the near-passive layers that fit them. Then build from there instead of chasing one perfect "passive" stream that someone online swears runs itself.

Questions people ask

Is a side hustle passive income?
Usually not. A side hustle typically pays for your time, so earnings stop when you stop working. Some side hustles can become more passive over time, for example by turning repeat work into reusable products.
What is truly passive income?
Very little income is fully passive. The closest examples are interest on savings, rental income through a manager, or near-passive options like cashback and a sponsored laptop sticker, and all of them need some setup.
Which should I start first?
If you need money soon or have more time than money, start with a side hustle. If you already own an unused asset or have savings, a passive stream may make sense.
Does passive income get taxed differently?
It can. Tax authorities may define passive income more narrowly than everyday usage. In the US, for example, the IRS has specific passive activity rules. Check official guidance or ask a tax professional.
Is Lidquid passive income or a side hustle?
It's close to passive. Once the removable sticker is on your laptop, there's nothing to do but work in public as usual. Earnings depend on how often your laptop is out.
How much passive income can I realistically make?
It varies widely with your situation, time and assets. Be skeptical of anyone promising a specific amount.
