Passive income ideas with no money: an honest list

By the Lidquid team · Updated October 16, 2026 · 4 min read

the short answer

You can start passive income with no money, but you'll pay in time instead. Ideas that need no cash, like digital products, content with affiliate links, or renting out things you already own, usually need hours of setup before they earn anything. The most genuinely passive no-cost options use what you already have: a parking spot, spare space, or the back of your laptop lid.

Can you really make passive income with no money?

Yes, but "no money" almost always means "lots of time". Passive income comes from an asset, and if you can't buy one, you have to build one or use one you already own.

That gives you two honest routes:

  • Use what you already own. This is the fastest route, with little work, because the asset already exists.
  • Build something once and sell it many times. Templates, photos, videos and articles are free to make but slow, and many never earn.

It's worth being honest with yourself about which route suits you. If you have more free evenings than possessions, building something can work, but expect months before it earns. If you're busy but own things that sit idle, renting them out will almost always beat starting a blog from scratch.

Each idea below is labelled truly passive, mostly passive or not passive, with what it takes up front. Earnings for all of them vary widely, so treat anyone quoting a guaranteed number with suspicion.

What are the best passive income ideas with no money?

Here are ten ideas that need no cash to start, roughly ordered from least to most upfront time.

1. Rent out ad space on your laptop lid (truly passive)

Up front: join a waitlist and approve a brand. If you already work in cafés, libraries or coworking spaces, your lid is facing a room full of people. Catch: it only earns if your laptop is out in public, and Lidquid is in pilot, opening one city at a time.

2. Rent out a parking spot or driveway (truly passive)

Up front: one listing on a parking app with access instructions. If you own a spot near offices, stations or venues, it can earn while you do nothing. Catch: leases and HOA rules often ban it, so check first.

3. Rent out storage space (mostly passive)

Up front: clear a garage corner, basement or closet and list it on a peer-to-peer storage marketplace. Catch: occasional access requests, plus insurance questions about other people's belongings.

4. Rent out gear you rarely use (mostly passive)

Up front: photos and listings for cameras, tools, camping or sports equipment. Catch: handovers, wear and damage risk. More in passive income from things you already own.

5. Sell digital templates (mostly passive, after setup)

Up front: many hours designing planners, spreadsheets, resume templates or slide decks with free tools, then listing them on a marketplace. Catch: crowded categories. Most templates sell rarely or never, and the winners usually solve a narrow, specific problem.

6. License your photos (mostly passive, after setup)

Up front: shooting, editing, tagging and submitting to stock photo sites. Your phone may be enough. Catch: high volume, strict review standards and modest earnings per image. It suits people who'd take the photos anyway.

7. Write evergreen articles with affiliate links (mostly passive, eventually)

Up front: a free blog and months of writing helpful content before search traffic arrives. Catch: slow, competitive and dependent on search rankings you don't control. Disclose affiliate links clearly.

8. Make evergreen videos (mostly passive, eventually)

Up front: recording, editing and publishing consistently until a channel qualifies for monetization. Catch: it's a lot of active work for a long time, and only some videos keep earning years later.

9. Refer people to services you already use (mostly passive)

Up front: sharing referral links for apps, banks or services you genuinely use. Catch: small and irregular, and it can annoy friends if you push it. Only share what you'd recommend anyway.

10. Sell things you don't use (not passive, but a starting point)

Up front: photos, listings and shipping. It's one-off income, not recurring, but it can turn into seed money for ideas that need a little cash, or clear space you can then rent out.

Which "no money" passive income ideas should you avoid?

Avoid anything that says it's free but asks for money before you've earned a cent. That's the classic shape of a scam.

  • "Free" programs that need a paid starter kit, course or membership
  • Get-paid-to-advertise offers that send you a cheque and ask you to send part of it back
  • Anything promising a fixed, high weekly income for no work
  • Schemes that mainly pay you for recruiting other people

Our guide to passive income scams walks through the red flags in detail.

About investing: dividends and interest are the textbook passive income, but they need money to start, which is outside this list. Once you have savings, learn the basics from neutral sources first. This isn't financial advice.

How should you start with zero budget?

Start with what you already own, because it earns fastest for the least effort. Then put spare time into one build-once idea.

  1. Audit your stuff: lid, driveway, garage, gear you never use.
  2. Set up the truly passive ones first, ideally in one afternoon.
  3. Pick one creative idea (templates, photos or writing) and commit to it for a few months instead of dabbling in five.
  4. Track time against money so you can tell whether something is really passive or quietly a part-time job. Our comparison of passive income vs side hustles helps with that.

Questions people ask

What is the easiest passive income with no money?
Using something you already own: renting a parking spot or storage space, or putting ad space on your laptop lid with a program like Lidquid. These need little setup and no cash.
Can I make passive income with no money and no skills?
Yes, mainly by renting out things you already own. Creative options like templates or photos need some skill, but you can learn as you go with free tools.
How long does it take to earn passive income from scratch?
Renting out existing assets can start quickly if there's demand. Built assets like blogs, videos or templates often take months, and some never earn, so start with the quick wins.
Is any passive income completely passive?
Very little. Almost everything needs setup and occasional upkeep. Ideas like laptop lid ad space or a self-access parking spot are among the closest to truly passive.
Are free passive income programs that charge a starter fee legit?
Treat them with suspicion. A genuine program pays you; it doesn't need you to pay first. Upfront fees and send-money-back cheques are classic scam signs.
