Is laptop sticker advertising legit, or a scam?

By the Lidquid team · Updated October 16, 2026 · 4 min read

the short answer

Laptop sticker advertising can be completely legitimate, but the idea is easy to fake. The test is simple: a real program pays you and never asks you to pay, cash a cheque or move money. If anyone asks for a fee, sends a cheque and wants part of it back, or won't name the brand, walk away.

  • Legit: free to join, the brand is named up front, you're paid per verified scan
  • Scam: upfront fees, overpayment cheques, guaranteed high weekly pay

Is getting paid to put a sticker on your laptop real?

Yes. Brands pay for attention in places where their customers spend time, and the back of an open laptop in a café or library gets a lot of it. The business model is the same as any outdoor advertising: a brand rents a surface that people look at.

What makes laptop stickers newer is measurement. A QR code can count every scan, so a brand can pay only when someone actually engages, rather than paying for a vague "impression." That's how Lidquid works. Brands pay per verified scan and laptop owners earn a share. Read how it works end to end for the full picture.

The trouble is that "get paid to advertise on your stuff" is also one of the oldest scam formats around, so it's worth knowing exactly what the fake version looks like.

What is the car wrap scam, and why does it matter here?

The car wrap scam promises you weekly payments for wrapping your car in an energy drink or other brand's ad, then uses a fake cheque to steal your money. The FTC and other consumer agencies have warned about it for years, and the same script adapts easily to laptop stickers.

It typically goes like this:

  1. You're contacted by text, email or social media, or you answer an ad promising easy money for advertising on your car.
  2. You're told you'll get a big, fixed weekly payment just for driving around as usual.
  3. A cheque arrives for more than your first payment. You're told to deposit it, keep your share and send the rest to an "installer" or "graphics company," often by gift card, wire transfer or a payment app.
  4. The cheque bounces days later. The money you sent is gone, and the bank holds you responsible.

Swap "car" for "laptop" and the script works just as well, which is why anything that involves you sending money should end the conversation immediately.

What are the red flags of a laptop sticker scam?

The biggest red flag is any request for you to pay or move money. A legitimate advertising program pays you, full stop. Watch for these warning signs:

  • Upfront fees. Sign-up fees, "starter kit" charges, listing fees with no guaranteed buyer, or a fee to "unlock" campaigns.
  • Overpayment cheques. Any cheque larger than what you're owed, followed by a request to send the difference somewhere.
  • Gift cards, crypto or wire transfers. No legitimate company asks for payment this way.
  • Guaranteed high weekly pay regardless of where your laptop goes or how many people see it.
  • No named brand. They won't tell you who you'd be advertising before you commit.
  • Pressure and urgency. "Only three spots left, reply today."
  • Unsolicited contact from someone you've never heard of, often from a personal email or messaging account.
  • Requests for sensitive details early on, like your bank login, full card number or ID documents before you've even seen an offer.
If you've already sent money: contact your bank or payment provider straight away, keep every message and receipt, and report it to your national consumer protection agency (in the US, that's the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov).

What questions should you ask before joining?

A real program will answer all of these clearly and in writing. If you get vague answers, treat that as your answer.

  • Do I ever pay you anything? The answer should be no.
  • Which brand would I be advertising, and can I see the design first?
  • How exactly am I paid? Per scan, per month, per campaign? What's the rate?
  • What counts toward my earnings? How are fake or repeat scans handled?
  • How do I get paid out, and when?
  • How do I stop? Is there a minimum term, a penalty or a fee to leave?
  • Will the sticker damage my laptop? What's it made of, and does it come off clean?

What does a legitimate laptop advertising program look like?

A legitimate program is transparent about the brand, the rate and how earnings are counted, and money only ever flows toward you. Specifically, you should expect:

  • A free sign-up with no purchase required.
  • The brand, sticker design and rate shown to you before anything ships.
  • The right to say no to any brand.
  • A clear definition of what earns money, such as verified scans.
  • Earnings that depend on real activity, not a suspiciously high fixed payment.
  • No minimum term and an easy way out.
  • A real website with clear terms, not just a messaging account.

Questions people ask

Is Lidquid a scam?
No. Lidquid is free for laptop owners, shows you the brand and rate before anything ships, and never asks you to pay, deposit a cheque or send money anywhere.
Why would someone send me a cheque for a sticker?
That's the classic overpayment scam. A real program pays you what you've earned. It never sends extra money and asks you to forward the difference.
Should I ever pay to get paid for advertising?
No. A legitimate advertising program pays you. Sign-up fees, starter-kit fees and gift card requests are all warning signs.
Are laptop sticker marketplaces that charge a listing fee scams?
Not necessarily, but a listing fee means you pay with no guarantee anyone buys. Read the terms carefully and know you could lose the fee.
What should I do if I think I've been scammed?
Contact your bank or payment provider right away, keep all messages and receipts, and report it to your consumer protection agency. In the US, report it to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
How do I know a laptop advertising offer is real?
A real offer names the brand, shows you the design and rate up front, explains how earnings are counted, costs you nothing and lets you leave at any time.
