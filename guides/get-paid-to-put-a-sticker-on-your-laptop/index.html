Can you get paid to put a sticker on your laptop?

By the Lidquid team · Updated October 16, 2026 · 4 min read

the short answer

Yes. Brands will pay to put a sticker on the back of your laptop because people in cafés, libraries, campuses and coworking spaces look at it all day. With Lidquid you stick on a removable QR sticker for a brand you approve, and you earn every time a new person scans it.

  • Cost to you: nothing. Legitimate programs never charge owners a fee.
  • Work: none beyond using your laptop in public like you already do. There's no posting and no follower count.
  • Earnings: paid per verified scan, so a lid that's out in public most days earns far more than one that stays at home.

Why would a brand pay for my laptop lid?

The back of an open laptop is one of the few blank surfaces that sits at eye level for hours in places full of people. Picture the person opposite you in a café, the queue behind you at the counter, the row behind you in a lecture hall. They all see your lid, and nobody is selling that attention yet.

Traditional out-of-home advertising like posters, bus shelters and flyers is expensive and hard to measure. A QR sticker on a laptop is the opposite. It's cheap to print, it goes wherever your target audience already sits, and every scan can be counted. That's what makes it worth paying for. A brand isn't buying "views". It's buying measurable moments where someone was curious enough to pull out their phone.

What counts as a "verified scan"?

A verified scan is a real person, on a real phone, scanning your sticker for the first time. Lidquid filters out:

  • repeat scans from the same device (your friend scanning ten times doesn't count ten times)
  • scans from your own phone
  • bot and automated traffic

This matters for you as well as for the brand. Paying only for genuine first-time scans is what lets brands trust the numbers, and that trust is what keeps campaigns, and payments, coming. Read more in what is a verified scan.

How much can you earn?

Earnings come from a simple formula:

monthly earnings ≈ days out in public × verified scans per day × rate per scan × 4.33

The brand sets the rate per scan and you see it before you accept. As an illustration only, a laptop that's out five days a week, picks up three new scans a day at $0.50 a scan, works out to roughly $32 a month. Busier spots, more days out and higher-rate brands push that up. A laptop that rarely leaves the house will earn close to nothing.

Honest note: Lidquid is still in pilot. We haven't published real earnings numbers yet and won't invent them. Real figures will be shared once the first city has run. Until then, treat any number (ours included) as an estimate. You can try different inputs in the earnings estimator or read the full earnings breakdown.

Who it works best for

Laptop advertising rewards dwell time in public. It suits you if you're:

  • a student who works in the library, student union or campus cafés
  • a remote worker or freelancer who works from cafés several days a week
  • a coworking member with a regular desk in a busy shared space
  • a commuter who works on trains, in airport lounges or in waiting rooms

It's a poor fit if your laptop stays at a home desk, or if your employer's device policy bans stickers on work machines. Check that before you put anything on a company laptop.

Will a sticker damage my laptop?

Not if it's the right material. Lidquid uses removable vinyl made for laptop lids. It doesn't block vents, it peels off without leaving residue, and it won't hurt your resale value. There's more detail in are laptop stickers bad for your laptop?

Red flags: how to spot a laptop sticker scam

"Get paid to put a sticker on your laptop" sounds a lot like the classic car wrap scam, so it's worth knowing the warning signs. Walk away if a program:

  • asks you to pay a sign-up, listing or "starter kit" fee
  • sends you a cheque and asks you to send part of it back or forward money to an "installer"
  • promises a fixed, high weekly payment no matter where your laptop goes
  • won't tell you which brand you'd be advertising before you commit

Lidquid never charges owners anything, never asks you to move money, and shows you the brand and rate before anything ships. For the full checklist, see is laptop sticker advertising legit?

Other ways people get paid for laptop space

Lidquid isn't the only take on the idea. Some sites let you list your lid on a marketplace and sell fixed-price sticker spots to companies, usually for a listing fee. Developers and creators have also auctioned sticker space on their own laptops, typically to fund a new machine. These one-off deals can work if you have a big following, because the sponsor is really paying for your audience. Lidquid is built for everyone else. You don't need an audience, because you're paid for what the people around you do. Our comparison of laptop, car and other "get paid to advertise" options covers the trade-offs.

Questions people ask

Do I need followers or a social media audience?
No. You're paid for verified scans from people around you in the real world, not for posting. There's no follower requirement and nothing to post.
Does it cost anything to join?
No. Joining the waitlist and taking part are free. A program that asks you to pay a fee, or to cash a cheque and send money back, is a scam.
Can I choose which brand goes on my laptop?
Yes. You see the brand, the sticker design and the rate per scan before anything ships, and you can turn down any brand for any reason.
Can I put a sponsored sticker on a work laptop?
Only if your employer allows it. Many company device policies forbid stickers or third-party branding on work machines, so check first or use a personal laptop.
What happens if I want to stop?
Peel the sticker off. There's no lock-in, no minimum term and no fee, and you keep everything you've already earned.
