## c) Three suggestions for what to make next

**1. Run the "cities open in order of demand" angle next, as a shareable pin.**
It's the only claim on the site with a built-in reason to *act today rather than later* — joining the waitlist is literally the vote, and the success state already hands people a referral link that bumps them up the queue. Every other angle explains the product; this one creates urgency and rides the existing referral mechanic. A pin like "your city opens when enough people want it" is also inherently shareable within a city, which is exactly the shape of demand the pilot needs (50 laptops, one city at a time — concentrated, not scattered).

**2. Make a plain-language "what counts as a verified scan" explainer graphic.**
The single biggest trust gap for a stranger hitting this cold is "so what stops someone scanning their own sticker 400 times?" The FAQ answers it well and drily ("you scanning it yourself in a mirror" doesn't count), but that answer is buried at the bottom of the page and invisible on social. A three-panel zine strip — real person / real phone / first time — turns the most sceptical question into the most charming asset, and it's honest content that needs no invented numbers.

**3. Build a reusable pin template in Canva rather than generating from scratch each day.**
Today's run burned its image step on a network block *and* had to pick a candidate blind, because thumbnails were unreachable too. A single brand-locked Canva template with one editable headline field would make the daily image a text swap instead of a generative gamble — cheaper, faster, visually consistent across the whole Pinterest board, and it removes the "did the AI mangle the copy?" review step entirely. Worth doing before the queue grows, since consistency compounds on Pinterest.
