## c) Three things to make next

**1. The "cities open by demand" pin — and make it a map, not a poster.**
The waitlist is the only CTA on the site, and the site's own framing ("joining the list literally is the vote") is the sharpest thing it says: it converts an email-capture into a small act of local rivalry. A pin built as a hand-drawn city leaderboard with blank ranks — no invented city names, no invented counts — gives people a reason to send it to someone in their city rather than just save it. It's the one angle where sharing *is* the conversion. Do this next.

**2. A "peels off clean" post aimed squarely at the objection, not the offer.**
The FAQ answers "will the sticker wreck my laptop?" first for a reason — it's the thought that kills the idea before the money argument ever lands. Most of the funnel loss probably happens there, silently, and no asset currently addresses it. A close-up peel sequence — corner lifting, bare lid, nothing left — with dry copy like "the whole demo" would work on Instagram and doubles as a reply people can paste when someone in the comments asks.

**3. A student / term-time variant, timed for the next few weeks.**
Late August into September is when students are moving into new cities and looking for income that doesn't cost them shifts — and a laptop that lives in a library or campus café is exactly the high-traffic lid the pilot wants. The angle writes itself ("your laptop already sits in the library for six hours a day"), it's seasonally timed rather than evergreen, and it recruits the density of lids in one place that makes a city worth opening. Worth doing before the window closes.
