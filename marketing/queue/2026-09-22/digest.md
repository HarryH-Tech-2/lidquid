## c) three things to make next

**1. The "wait, what is that?" pin — the social angle, as a two-panel strip.**
The landing page already leans on this ("Quiet café regulars turn into people you actually know"), but nothing in the marketing queue does yet. It's the only angle where the benefit isn't money, which makes it the natural contrast to today's pin and gives the feed something other than side-hustle content. Pinterest rewards a pin you can read in one glance — two hand-drawn panels, someone leaning over, one line of dialogue — and it reaches a save-worthy "café life" audience rather than a "make money online" one.

**2. A "cities open in order of demand — vote with your city" pin, built to be re-shot per city.**
This is the site's actual mechanic and the closest thing to genuine urgency the product has without inventing numbers: joining the list *is* the vote. Make it as a template with a swappable city name on a hand-drawn ballot/pin-on-map motif, so it can run repeatedly without feeling like a repost. It also drives the second form field (city), which is where the waitlist gets its ordering signal — worth more per signup than a generic join.

**3. A LinkedIn post written for the brand side, not the laptop owner.**
Every draft so far talks to the person with the laptop, but the for-brands section and the pilot-pricing form are the half of the funnel with money in it, and LinkedIn is the only one of the three channels where media buyers actually are. Lead with the honest distinction the page already makes — pay for verified scans, not impressions nobody can prove, on placements the owner said yes to. Keep it to the claims on the page; no rates, no fake case studies.
