## c) three things to make next

1. **The "wait, what is that?" pin — the social angle.**
   The landing page calls this "the part nobody expects", and it's the one claim that isn't about
   money at all: the sticker turns quiet café regulars into people you actually know. That makes it
   the strongest counter-programme to tomorrow's likely-similar side-hustle pins, and it reaches
   people who scroll past income content. It also has an obvious visual: two laptops at
   neighbouring tables, one speech bubble.

2. **A "vote with your city" post aimed at a single named city, as a repeatable template.**
   Cities open in order of waitlist demand, which means the waitlist has a built-in local-rivalry
   mechanic that nothing in the queue uses yet. One post per city, same skeleton, city name swapped,
   is the cheapest way to turn one draft into many — and it converts on the exact CTA the site has
   (email + city). Worth building as a template file rather than a one-off.

3. **A "what counts as a verified scan" explainer card.**
   It's the load-bearing claim on both sides of the marketplace: it's why owners get paid fairly and
   why brands trust the numbers. It's also the most likely honest objection ("so what stops me
   scanning it myself?"), and the site already answers it with a joke about mirrors. An explainer
   that answers a real objection tends to outlive a hook-led pin, and it gives the brand-side
   audience something to forward.
