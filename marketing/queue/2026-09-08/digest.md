## (c) Three things to make next

**1. The "you approve every brand" pin — because it answers the objection that kills signups.**
The instinctive reaction to "rent your laptop lid" is *"so a company picks what goes on my
laptop?"*. The site already answers it well ("It's your lid; we're just the agent"), but no
marketing asset leads with it. A pin built on approval — a hand-drawn yes/no checkbox, the
headline "you can say no. for any reason." — turns the biggest hesitation into the hook. Highest
value of the three, and worth running early rather than saving.

**2. A city-voting post aimed at one specific city — because it's the only asset that compounds.**
Cities open in order of waitlist demand, which makes "joining is the vote" a genuine call to
action rather than a slogan, and it gives people a reason to send the link to someone else in
their city. Same template, swap the city name, run it wherever signups are already clustering.
This is the one piece of content that gets *better* the more the waitlist grows. (No numbers, no
"X people in Bristol already" — none of that is known.)

**3. A "peel test" short video — because the material claim is the one people won't take on
faith.** "Removable vinyl, zero residue" is a promise about someone's £1,500 laptop, and text
can't carry it. Fifteen seconds of a thumbnail lifting a corner and the sticker coming away
clean does what a paragraph can't, and it's the format Pinterest and Reels both favour. Needs a
real sticker and a real laptop, so it's a shoot request, not a drafting task — flagging it now
so it can be filmed when the pilot stickers exist.
